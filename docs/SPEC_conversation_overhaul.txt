OMERTÀ: Comprehensive NPC Interaction & Conversation Overhaul

Objective

I want a widespread, systemic overhaul of every NPC interaction and conversation function in OMERTÀ.

The objective is to transform the current interaction system from a collection of dialogue menus, information queries and isolated actions into a coherent, immersive, reactive social gameplay system.

This is not simply a dialogue-writing exercise.

I want you to reconsider the architecture, interaction flow, presentation, information disclosure, character behaviour, consequences and relationships across the entire game.

The ultimate goal is to make talking to people one of the most compelling parts of OMERTÀ.

The player should feel they’re dealing with independent people who have personalities, ambitions, loyalties, fears, grudges, responsibilities and their own understanding of the criminal world.

The underlying simulation is already sophisticated. The interaction system needs to expose that sophistication through believable human behaviour.

Do not settle for rewriting dialogue while leaving the underlying experience fundamentally unchanged.

⸻

1. Overhaul Every Interaction Function

Audit and redesign the complete NPC interaction system, including:

* Initial encounters and greetings
* Casual conversation
* Personal relationships
* Friendship and familiarity
* Business discussions
* Earnings, tribute and kickbacks
* Loans and debts
* Protection arrangements
* Business acquisitions and ownership
* Crew management
* Recruitment
* Orders and assignments
* Favours and obligations
* Promises and commitments
* Grievances and disputes
* Sit-downs and negotiations
* Threats and intimidation
* Apologies and making amends
* Family politics
* Relationships with superiors
* Relationships with subordinates
* Relationships with rival organisations
* Rumours and intelligence gathering
* Promotions and advancement
* Initiation and becoming made
* Leadership and succession
* Any other NPC-facing functionality currently implemented

Do not limit the overhaul to incidents or the conversation screen.

Every interaction should be reviewed for its purpose, naturalness, contextual relevance and connection to the underlying simulation.

Preserve the actual capabilities of the existing systems. The overhaul must improve how they are experienced, not silently remove them.

2. Introduce a Natural Conversation Lifecycle

Every interaction should follow a believable conversational progression.

Stage A: Encounter

The player approaches someone.

The NPC reacts according to their relationship, personality, current circumstances, location and recent history.

An old friend might greet the player warmly.

A superior might acknowledge them briefly.

An enemy might be suspicious.

Someone occupied with urgent business might have little patience.

The encounter should establish a situation, not immediately reveal everything the character knows.

Stage B: Opening Conversation

The player begins with natural conversational choices.

Examples might include:

* How’ve you been?
* How’s business?
* What’s been happening?
* Got a minute?
* I wanted to ask you something.

These are examples, not mandatory universal options.

The actual choices should depend on who the player is speaking to and why.

Do not force artificial pleasantries when the situation warrants getting straight to business.

Stage C: Discovery

Information emerges through what the player asks and what the NPC chooses to reveal.

If I ask what’s been happening, the NPC might mention a recent disagreement, a business opportunity, a rumour or something personal.

They might also have nothing interesting to say.

They should not automatically disclose every relevant fact.

Their willingness to speak should depend on their knowledge, trust, temperament, rank, loyalties, personal involvement and the sensitivity of the information.

Stage D: Development

Once a subject is introduced, the conversation develops naturally.

The next options must follow from the actual response.

For example:

NPC: “That hardware shop’s paying me now.”

Player: “Wasn’t somebody else collecting there?”

NPC: “Not anymore.”

Player: “What happened to him?”

NPC: “We had a conversation. He understood.”

This is the kind of continuity I want.

Each answer creates the next logical question.

Do not simply return to a generic list of questions after every response.

Stage E: Resolution

The conversation may conclude with:

* Nothing beyond a social exchange
* Improved or damaged relations
* New information
* A lead worth pursuing
* An agreement
* A promise
* A favour
* A debt
* A warning
* A threat
* An assignment
* A dispute
* A negotiation
* A change in someone’s intentions
* A meaningful refusal

Not every conversation needs a reward or dramatic outcome.

Ordinary conversation should remain possible.

However, meaningful conversations must have the potential to influence the world.

3. Make Dialogue Truly Contextual

The dialogue system must consider the complete relevant context.

At minimum:

* NPC personality and temperament
* Trust, warmth, respect and resentment
* Rank and organisational position
* Relative power between the characters
* Current location
* Recent events
* Personal involvement
* Relationships with third parties
* Organisational loyalties
* Financial interests
* Existing promises and obligations
* Active disputes
* What the NPC knows
* What the player knows
* What has already been discussed
* Whether the NPC has a reason to lie or withhold information

The same question should produce meaningfully different responses depending on these factors.

A trusted friend should not speak like a hostile capo.

A boss should not negotiate with an associate as though they have equal authority.

A man personally responsible for an incident should not respond identically to someone who heard about it second-hand.

Avoid treating personalities as cosmetic dialogue variations. They should affect behaviour, disclosure, negotiation and consequences.

4. Implement Persistent Conversational Memory

NPCs must remember meaningful interactions.

This includes:

* Information they’ve already disclosed
* Promises made
* Promises broken
* Favours performed
* Requests refused
* Threats issued
* Accusations made
* Previous negotiations
* Important personal conversations
* Past conflicts
* Attempts to deceive or pressure them

A character should not repeatedly reveal the same information as though it were new.

If I promised to help someone and failed, that history should influence future encounters.

If I repeatedly ask a suspicious character about something sensitive, they may become guarded or irritated.

Distinguish between temporary conversation state and persistent social memory.

Not every line needs permanent storage. Persist what matters, using bounded and save-compatible records.

5. Eliminate Repetition and Exploitation

An option already used in the current conversation should not repeatedly produce the same reward.

Implement the following:

* Remember used options within the current encounter.
* Disable or remove exhausted choices where appropriate.
* Prevent repeated selections from generating additional trust, warmth, respect, standing, money or other benefits.
* Avoid rewarding the same casual conversation repeatedly throughout the day.
* Preserve meaningful opportunities to revisit subjects when circumstances actually change.
* Allow a new conversation to begin naturally without pretending the previous conversation never happened.

Do not rely solely on greyed-out buttons.

Where possible, the dialogue itself should acknowledge that a subject has already been discussed.

For example:

“We already talked about that.”

Or:

“Nothing’s changed since you asked me yesterday.”

These responses should fit the character and situation.

6. Redesign Dialogue Options Around Intent

The player should not routinely face ten or fifteen questions.

Use a smaller number of meaningful choices that represent distinct intentions.

For example:

* Learn more
* Challenge what was said
* Offer help
* Ask for something
* Change the subject

These are conceptual categories, not universal labels.

The actual wording must be natural and contextual.

More specific options should appear as the conversation develops.

The player should have substantial agency without being presented with every possible action simultaneously.

Reduce visible complexity without reducing underlying optionality.

Actions should become available because the situation makes them relevant.

Do not bury urgent or meaningful decisions behind unnecessary layers of menus.

7. Make Information Disclosure Credible

The player must discover the world through believable sources.

Maintain strict distinctions between:

* What objectively happened
* What an NPC personally witnessed
* What an NPC heard
* What an NPC believes
* What an NPC suspects
* What an NPC deliberately conceals
* What the player actually knows

NPCs should never reveal information simply because the simulation contains it.

A character can be mistaken.

A rumour can be incomplete.

Someone can refuse to answer.

Someone can deliberately misrepresent their involvement when their motives justify it.

However, do not invent contradictory facts without recording the belief, deception or uncertainty appropriately.

The simulation must remain authoritative.

8. Remove Database Language From Gameplay

Eliminate interface wording that exposes internal records instead of presenting believable dialogue.

Examples of unacceptable conversational language include:

* “The matter”
* “Which incident?”
* “Something from Day 1 10:02”
* “He knows this first-hand”
* Generic descriptions of records
* Repeated references to internal event categories

Do not display a complete incident summary before the NPC has actually disclosed it.

The player should experience conversations, not browse a database of known facts.

Technical metadata can remain available in development and testing tools.

Where information needs to be reviewed later, present it naturally through a journal, remembered conversation or relevant character history.

9. Give Every Character a Distinct Voice

Improve dialogue generation across the entire interaction system.

Characters should vary in:

* Directness
* Vocabulary
* Humour
* Patience
* Confidence
* Aggression
* Caution
* Willingness to gossip
* Deference to authority
* Familiarity with the player

Dialogue must remain believable for New York’s criminal underworld in 1973.

Avoid exaggerated gangster stereotypes, repetitive catchphrases and theatrical dialogue.

Characters should use first-person language naturally.

A man should not ordinarily refer to himself by his full name.

Avoid mechanically repeating the NPC’s name at the start of every paragraph.

The goal is convincing characterisation, not simply more colourful text.

10. Make Social Relationships Mechanically Meaningful

Relationships should affect what players can accomplish.

Trust should influence disclosure and willingness to cooperate.

Respect should influence whether requests are taken seriously.

Warmth should influence personal familiarity and generosity.

Fear, resentment and rivalry should influence reactions where those states exist in the simulation.

Relationships should develop through meaningful shared experiences, not repetitive conversation farming.

A player who helps someone through a serious dispute should earn a different kind of loyalty from someone who simply makes pleasant conversation.

Characters should also remember negative behaviour.

Do not introduce arbitrary relationship rewards without a defensible causal basis.

11. Integrate Conversations With Autonomous NPC Behaviour

This is essential.

The game already contains autonomous organisations, relationships, disputes, business interests, financial obligations and NPC ambitions.

Conversations must be a meaningful interface to those systems.

An NPC might:

* Ask the player for help
* Seek protection
* Request money
* Complain about another character
* Reveal dissatisfaction with a superior
* Offer a business opportunity
* Warn about a rival
* Seek a promotion
* Refuse an order
* Ask for a favour
* Attempt to manipulate the player
* Conceal a financial problem
* Propose an arrangement

These situations must originate from actual world state.

Do not manufacture artificial quests solely to populate dialogue.

The NPC should continue pursuing their interests even if the player does nothing.

A promise, agreement, threat or intervention should feed back into the real simulation.

12. Make Rank and Power Matter

The experience of conversation must change substantially as the player advances.

An associate should not interact with a boss in the same way a capo does.

A soldier should have different responsibilities and opportunities from a crew leader.

A capo should be able to discuss organisational matters, issue instructions and handle disputes appropriate to their authority.

A boss should be able to negotiate with other leaders, manage subordinates and deal with political consequences.

The available dialogue, information, requests and responses should evolve with rank.

Make advancement feel like entering a different social and political world, not simply unlocking another title.

13. Improve Negotiations and Difficult Conversations

High-stakes interactions should feel meaningfully different from casual conversation.

This includes:

* Debt negotiations
* Protection arrangements
* Business deals
* Sit-downs
* Threats
* Requests for promotion
* Disputes between crews
* Inter-family disagreements
* Broken promises
* Challenges to authority

Characters should have objectives, acceptable outcomes, leverage and reasons to resist.

The player should be able to negotiate through meaningful choices.

NPCs should respond according to their interests rather than following predetermined dialogue sequences.

Consequences must update the authoritative economic, organisational and social records.

Avoid creating duplicate disputes, promises or obligations.

14. Overhaul the Conversation Interface

The current mobile presentation also needs improvement.

There is excessive empty space above character portraits.

Conversations can still resemble lists of buttons rather than exchanges between people.

Redesign the interface so that:

* The NPC and current exchange are immediately visible.
* The screen makes efficient use of mobile space.
* Dialogue is the visual focus.
* Available responses are clearly distinguished from previous dialogue.
* The conversation progresses without unnecessary scrolling.
* Previous exchanges can be reviewed when useful.
* Important decisions are visually distinct from ordinary questions.
* Options change naturally as the discussion progresses.
* Leaving, returning and changing subjects are intuitive.

Avoid clutter, excessive nested menus and repetitive panels.

The interface should feel like an interactive narrative RPG, not a management dashboard.

Preserve all existing character portraits exactly as they are.

Do not redraw, regenerate, replace or restyle them.

15. Preserve the Existing Simulation

This overhaul must build upon V72–V76, not replace them.

Preserve:

* Existing saves and migration compatibility
* Economic records and authoritative transactions
* NPC autonomy
* Criminal organisations
* Leadership and succession
* Business ownership and protection
* Debts and obligations
* Disputes and grievances
* Sit-down outcomes
* Promises and consequences
* Character relationships
* Knowledge restrictions
* Rank progression
* Existing meaningful player actions

Do not introduce a runtime AI dependency.

The game must remain a fully functional standalone browser application.

You may refactor the conversation architecture where necessary, but do not destabilise the underlying simulation.

16. Comprehensive Automated Testing

Build an automated interaction crawler capable of exploring the redesigned conversation system.

Test across:

* Different NPC personalities
* Different trust levels
* Different ranks
* Different relationships
* Different organisations
* Ordinary and high-stakes encounters
* Characters with and without relevant information
* Active and resolved disputes
* Existing promises and debts
* Multiple encounters with the same NPC
* Save and reload cycles

Check for:

* Crashes
* Dead ends
* Broken navigation
* Empty dialogue
* Repeated dialogue
* Repeated rewards
* Inappropriate questions
* Contradictory responses
* Information leaks
* Characters referring to themselves unnaturally
* Inaccessible gameplay actions
* Duplicate economic or dispute records
* Promises that do not persist
* Actions that fail to affect the simulation
* Incorrect rank permissions
* Broken save compatibility

Do not rely exclusively on technical assertions.

Include semantic checks for conversational continuity and contextual appropriateness.

The crawler should help identify problems, but real gameplay review remains essential.

Use efficient, targeted testing. Do not waste time on endless large-scale simulations when smaller reproducible tests will establish correctness.

17. Demonstrate the Actual Experience

Before declaring this complete, demonstrate full conversations from real game states.

I want to see at least:

1. An ordinary friendly conversation with no significant events.
2. A guarded conversation where an NPC initially refuses to disclose information.
3. A conversation where the player gradually uncovers a meaningful incident.
4. A business negotiation with competing interests.
5. A conversation involving an existing promise or grievance.
6. An interaction with a superior where rank and trust matter.
7. An interaction with a subordinate where the player exercises authority.
8. A conversation where an earlier interaction meaningfully changes the outcome.

Show complete exchanges, not isolated dialogue snippets.

For each, explain which existing simulation records were used and what, if anything, changed.

I want evidence that the player is interacting with a living world, not selecting from a collection of prewritten questions.

18. Completion Criteria

This overhaul is complete only when:

* Conversations begin naturally.
* Information is disclosed progressively and credibly.
* Dialogue responds to conversational history.
* Characters have distinct personalities.
* Questions follow logically from preceding answers.
* Important interactions have meaningful consequences.
* Relationships influence behaviour.
* Rank influences authority and access.
* NPCs remember significant interactions.
* Repeated conversations cannot be exploited.
* The interface is comfortable and immersive on mobile.
* Existing gameplay functionality remains accessible.
* Autonomous world systems remain authoritative.
* Automated tests pass.
* Real transcripts demonstrate believable conversations.

Implementation Approach

First, audit the existing interaction architecture and identify the common causes of the problems.

Then implement a coherent system-wide solution rather than applying isolated patches to individual dialogue branches.

Use shared conversational principles and reusable mechanisms wherever appropriate.

Do not create an enormous collection of special-case scripts that become impossible to maintain.

You have authority to refactor the interaction system substantially where necessary.

However, preserve the existing simulation, game functionality, save compatibility and all character portraits.

Work through implementation, testing and refinement systematically.

Do not stop at a design document or a prototype.

Implement the overhaul across the actual playable game.

At completion, provide:

* A concise explanation of the architectural changes.
* A summary of the interaction functions updated.
* Real transcripts demonstrating the new behaviour.
* Regression and crawler results.
* Any remaining limitations or known issues.

Then publish the updated playable version for review.

Final Principle

OMERTÀ should not feel like a game where I click on people to retrieve information and select transactions.

It should feel like a world where I build relationships, navigate loyalties, discover opportunities, negotiate power and make decisions through interactions with people who have their own lives and interests.

The simulation creates the world. The conversations should make that world feel alive.

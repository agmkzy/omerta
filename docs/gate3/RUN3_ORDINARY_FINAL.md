## What the month shows

- **Test A, Richie Amato.** Over the month Charles learns:
  - what Richie wants (to be "somebody they ask before they decide");
  - that his racket is thin ("Odd jobs, and fewer of them");
  - that he will pass on what he hears.

  Richie trusts Charles with a delivery job. Greetings remember the last evening.

  What does not develop is warmth. Their temperaments don't match, so the evenings bring familiarity but not trust or affection. Four of the ten evenings had nothing fresh on his mind and were about the fights or the Yankees.
- **Test C, independent concerns.** These came to Charles unprompted, from the men's own circumstances:
  - Mickey Sorrento selling a tip;
  - Richie's delivery;
  - Frankie Lombardi's wedding.
- **Test E, ordinary play:**
  - protection put to three shops (a counter at $50, a flat "No. How would I know you'll keep your end?", a refusal remembered on the return visit);
  - advancement asked about three times, with the answer changing as Charles's reputation changed ("unproven", then "trusted");
  - a sit-down offered between Teddy and Lou Fontana after the wedding gossip.
- **Repetition.**
  - Teddy's "Nothing now. Don't look for work. It finds you." twice.
  - "Nobody puts a name forward on what you've passed up so far." three times, from the old engine, unchanged.
  - Richie's guarded "Teddy? Never a dull day." twice, the second time when the routine asked again.

Phase 3: Trace Consequences Through the Simulation

For each significant player decision, identify:

1. Immediate outcome: What happened during the interaction?
2. Recorded state: What changed in the underlying game?
3. Subsequent behaviour: Did that change influence later dialogue, opportunities, autonomous NPC decisions or economic activity?
4. Long-term outcome: Did anything meaningful develop over the following days or weeks?

Crucially, distinguish between:

A. Confirmed integration: A recorded consequence demonstrably affects another system.

B. Potential integration: The code contains a plausible connection, but the test did not trigger it.

C. Missing integration: A consequence is recorded but no relevant downstream system appears to use it.

D. Legitimate inactivity: The system can react, but the character reasonably takes no action under the circumstances.

E. Defect: The game behaves inconsistently with its own rules.

Do not automatically classify an uneventful outcome as a failure. A character holding a grudge without immediately retaliating may be entirely believable.

However, distinguish believable restraint from a grudge variable that is never consulted by autonomous behaviour.

Use code inspection where necessary to establish the difference.

* Repeated conversations respect persistent memory.
* Decisions carry meaningful risks, obligations or consequences.

Pay particular attention to negotiations that resolve too easily, generic refusals and characters accepting offers without a convincing reason.

Do not confuse a passing automated test suite with a compelling gameplay experience.
